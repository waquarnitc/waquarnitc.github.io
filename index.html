Research roadmap · November 2026

Speech technology that works for every voice.

A research agenda built around atypical and under-represented speech: dysarthric speakers, children, low-resource conditions, and reliable voice biometrics. The aim is to move from isolated accuracy gains toward systems that generalize, explain their errors, protect users, and work with limited data.

01 · Established strengths

Research areas visible in the publication record

The linked profile was not fully retrievable directly because Google Scholar rate-limited access. These themes are based on publication records cross-checked against independent scholarly indexes and publisher pages; they should be treated as a verified sample, not a complete profile audit.

Core theme

Dysarthric speaker verification

Determine whether two speech recordings come from the same speaker when articulation, phonation, rhythm, and intelligibility differ from typical speech.

  • Voice biometrics for accessibility
  • Speaker embeddings and temporal cues
  • Severity-aware evaluation
Core theme

Acoustic features and fusion

Explore which parts of the speech spectrum and prosody remain useful when conventional assumptions about speech production break down.

  • MFCC, LFCC, CQCC and multitaper features
  • Prosodic and spectral fusion
  • High-frequency information
Core theme

Speech data augmentation

Increase effective training diversity when labelled speech from the target population is scarce.

  • Duration and rate modification
  • Voice-conversion augmentation
  • Out-of-domain training data
Related theme

Children's speech processing

Address acoustic mismatch between adult-trained systems and children's speech, where pitch, vocal-tract size, pronunciation and language use vary with age.

  • Low-/zero-resource ASR
  • Children's speaker verification
  • Age-aware adaptation
Related theme

Robust speech analysis

Use signal-processing representations and front ends to improve robustness under noise and difficult acoustic conditions.

  • Bispectrum-based features
  • Vowel detection
  • Filterbank design for high-pitched voices
Opportunity

Modern speech foundation models

Connect established signal-processing knowledge with self-supervised models such as wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT and WavLM.

  • Parameter-efficient adaptation
  • Feature-layer selection
  • Small-data and CPU-friendly systems

02 · Proposed research directions

Six research problems worth investigating

Each idea is deliberately framed as a testable question with a plausible baseline, evaluation protocol, and potential contribution. Check recent work and dataset licences before claiming novelty or starting experiments.

PROBLEM 01 · HIGH PRIORITY

Cross-corpus generalization for dysarthric speaker verification

Question: Can a verifier trained on one corpus retain identity information on an unseen corpus, microphone, or recording session—without confusing dysarthria severity with identity?

Domain generalizationSpeaker verificationHigh feasibility
  • Train on one corpus and test on a disjoint corpus where licensing permits.
  • Compare MFCC/LFCC/CQCC, x-vector or ECAPA-style embeddings, and SSL embeddings.
  • Report EER, minDCF, calibration, and results by severity / session / channel.
  • Test whether augmentation improves unseen-domain performance, not just in-domain scores.

Potential contribution: a reproducible cross-corpus protocol and severity-aware robustness analysis.

PROBLEM 02 · HIGH IMPACT

Few-shot personalized speech recognition for dysarthria

Question: How many minutes of a person's speech are needed to meaningfully improve recognition, and can adaptation avoid overfitting to a small vocabulary?

PersonalizationSSL / PEFTAccessibility
  • Compare frozen encoders, adapters/LoRA, and full fine-tuning.
  • Use speaker-disjoint evaluation and learning curves at several data budgets.
  • Report WER, intelligibility-sensitive error types, latency, and per-speaker variation.
  • Measure whether a language model corrects errors or hallucinates plausible words.

Potential contribution: a data-budget and adaptation recipe that can be reproduced by other labs.

PROBLEM 03 · DISTINCTIVE

Identity-preserving augmentation with quality control

Question: Which synthetic transformations add useful dysarthric variation while preserving speaker identity and clinically meaningful speech characteristics?

Data augmentationVoice conversionEvaluation toolkit
  • Compare rate/duration changes, spectral transforms and voice-conversion samples.
  • Score synthetic data for speaker similarity, phonetic coverage and artefacts.
  • Train with quality-filtered versus unfiltered synthetic data.
  • Run ablations to separate data volume from data quality.

Potential contribution: an augmentation acceptance test based on downstream benefit, not visual/audio plausibility alone.

PROBLEM 04 · CHILD SPEECH

Age-robust, low-resource children's ASR

Question: Can adult-pretrained speech models be adapted to children across age bands and accents using little labelled child speech?

Children's ASRLow-resourceTransfer learning
  • Compare zero-shot, layer selection, adapters/LoRA, and small-data fine-tuning.
  • Use age- and speaker-disjoint splits to avoid leakage.
  • Report WER by age, accent, noise and utterance length.
  • Compare synthetic child-like speech carefully; synthetic data must not replace held-out real-child evaluation.

Potential contribution: an age-aware adaptation strategy and an honest benchmark of data efficiency.

PROBLEM 05 · TRUST & SAFETY

Calibrated and spoof-aware voice biometrics for atypical speech

Question: How reliable are dysarthric speaker verification scores under replay, voice conversion, generated speech, and out-of-domain conditions?

Anti-spoofingCalibrationSecurity
  • Build a threat model before collecting or synthesizing attack samples.
  • Evaluate genuine, impostor, replay and synthetic trials separately.
  • Measure EER alongside minDCF, calibration and attack-specific error rates.
  • Study accessible fallback paths when confidence is low; never treat a score as proof of identity.

Potential contribution: a threat-aware evaluation protocol for voice biometrics involving atypical speech.

PROBLEM 06 · SIGNAL PROCESSING × SSL

Explainable acoustic–foundation-model fusion

Question: Do interpretable cues—prosody, spectral tilt, cepstral features, voice quality—complement SSL embeddings, and under which conditions?

Feature fusionInterpretabilitySignal processing
  • Compare handcrafted features, SSL layers, score fusion and learned fusion.
  • Use controlled perturbations and feature ablations to test what the model relies on.
  • Analyse robustness to noise, channel, rate and severity.
  • Track model size, CPU latency and memory use alongside accuracy.

Potential contribution: evidence-based fusion rather than adding features without explaining their value.

03 · From idea to paper

A practical 12-week pilot

A manageable starting point is Problem 01 or Problem 03 because both build directly on the existing dysarthric speaker-verification line and can begin with public datasets, subject to each dataset's terms.

Weeks 1–2 · Verify

Read the closest papers, write a one-page gap statement, verify data access and ethics requirements, and define the target claim.

Weeks 3–4 · Baseline

Reproduce a simple baseline; lock speaker-disjoint splits and preprocessing; record compute and data provenance.

Weeks 5–8 · Test

Run one main intervention and targeted ablations. Use multiple seeds where feasible and preserve a held-out test set.

Weeks 9–12 · Validate

Analyse failures and subgroup performance, test robustness, document limitations, and prepare a reproducible paper/code release.
